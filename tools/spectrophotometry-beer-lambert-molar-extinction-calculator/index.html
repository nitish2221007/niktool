Absorbance is directly proportional to concentration ($A = arepsilon b c$); quantifying protein concentrations (NanoDrop $A_{280}$), nucleic acids ($A_{260}$), and chemical dye solutions forms the backbone of quantitative analytical chemistry.

Frequently asked questions

Why do absorbance readings above A = 2.0 become inaccurate?

At $A = 2.0$, only 1% of light is transmitted; detector stray light and electronic noise dominate, causing non-linear deviation from Beer's law.
