How to use Coaxial Cable Characteristic Impedance & Attenuation Calculator

  1. Enter the inner conductor diameter ($d$) in millimeters.
  2. Enter the outer conductor inner diameter ($D$) in millimeters.
  3. Enter the dielectric relative permittivity ($\epsilon_r$, e.g., 2.25 for polyethylene).
  4. Click Process to compute $Z_0$, capacitance, velocity factor, and cutoff frequency.

Mathematical & Theoretical Formulas

This calculator evaluates performance using the following core formulas:

$$\displaystyle Z_0 = \frac{138}{\sqrt{\epsilon_r}} \log_{10}\left(\frac{D}{d}\right) \Omega, \quad C = \frac{24.15 \cdot \epsilon_r}{\log_{10}(D/d)} \text{ pF/m}$$

Frequently asked questions

Why is 50 Ohms the RF coaxial standard?

50 Ohms provides the optimal balance between maximum power handling capability (at ~30 Ω) and minimum attenuation loss (at ~77 Ω).

What is Velocity Factor (VF)?

Velocity factor is the speed of an electromagnetic wave in the cable divided by the speed of light in vacuum.

What causes cable cutoff frequency?

At high frequencies, higher-order non-TEM waveguide modes (like TE11) start propagating, degrading signal fidelity.
