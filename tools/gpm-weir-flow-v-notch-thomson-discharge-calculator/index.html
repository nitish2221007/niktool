How to use V-Notch (Thomson Triangular) Open-Channel Weir Discharge (Q) Calculator

  1. Enter measured liquid head height H above the V-notch apex vertex in centimeters.
  2. Select triangular notch opening angle $ heta$ (90°, 60°, or 45°).
  3. Enter discharge coefficient $C_d$ (typically 0.58 for sharp-crested thin-plate weirs).
  4. Inspect open-channel volumetric flow discharge rate in L/s, US GPM, and $m^3/ ext{hour}$.

James Thomson 1858 Triangular Weir Hydraulics

Triangular V-notch weirs maintain geometric self-similarity at all water depths ($Q propto H^{5/2}$), providing far greater flow measurement accuracy at low flow rates than rectangular weirs in environmental stream monitoring and wastewater effluent channels.

Frequently asked questions

Why is a 90° V-notch the most popular open-channel weir?

At 90°, $ an( heta/2) = an(45^circ) = 1.0$, simplifying the discharge equation to $Q approx 1.38 H^{2.5} ext{ m}^3/ ext{s}$.
