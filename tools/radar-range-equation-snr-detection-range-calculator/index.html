How to use Radar Range Equation & Detection Range Calculator

  1. Enter radar peak transmitter power $P_t$ in kilowatts (kW).
  2. Enter radar antenna gain $G$ in dB.
  3. Enter target Radar Cross Section (RCS $\sigma$) in square meters ($m^2$).
  4. Enter operating frequency in GHz and minimum detectable signal $P_{min}$ in dBm.
  5. Click Process to compute maximum radar detection range $R_{max}$.

Mathematical & Theoretical Formulas

This calculator evaluates performance using the following core formulas:

$$\displaystyle R_{\text{max}} = \left[ \frac{P_t \cdot G^2 \cdot \lambda^2 \cdot \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 \cdot P_{\text{min}}} \right]^{1/4}$$

Frequently asked questions

Why is radar range governed by a fourth-root law?

EM waves attenuate by 1/R² going to the target, and reflected echoes attenuate by another 1/R² returning, yielding overall 1/R⁴ two-way loss.

To double radar range, how much power increase is required?

Due to the 4th-root relationship, doubling detection range requires 2⁴ = 16 times (12 dB) more transmitter power!

What is Radar Cross Section (RCS)?

RCS measures how detectable an object is by radar; stealth aircraft aim to minimize RCS.
