How to use RSA Public-Key Cryptography (Key Generation, Encryption c = m^e mod n & Decryption) Calculator

  1. Enter two distinct prime numbers p and q (e.g. 61 and 53).
  2. Enter public encryption exponent e coprime to $phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)$ (commonly 17 or 65537).
  3. Enter numeric plaintext integer message m ($m < n$).
  4. Inspect generated RSA Public Key $(e, n)$, Private Key $(d, n)$, encrypted ciphertext c, and restored plaintext decryption.

Rivest, Shamir & Adleman 1977 Public-Key Cryptosystem

RSA security relies on the hardness of prime integer factorization ($n = p cdot q$); while computing $n$ is instantaneous, factoring a 2048-bit $n$ back into $p$ and $q$ would take classical supercomputers trillions of years.

Frequently asked questions

Why is e = 65537 (2^16 + 1) the standard public exponent in modern TLS/HTTPS?

65537 in binary has only two "1" bits ($10000000000000001_2$), enabling fast modular exponentiation in just 17 multiplications.
