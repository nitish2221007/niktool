How to use Tokamak Safety Factor (q_a & Kruskal-Shafranov Limit) Calculator

  1. Enter tokamak major radius R in meters.
  2. Enter plasma minor radius a in meters.
  3. Enter central toroidal magnetic field $B_T$ in Tesla.
  4. Enter driven plasma current $I_p$ in Mega-Amperes (MA).
  5. Enter plasma cross-sectional vertical elongation ratio $kappa = b/a$ (e.g. 1.7 for D-shaped plasma).
  6. Inspect calculated edge safety factor $q_a$ and verify whether the plasma is safe from the Kruskal-Shafranov external kink limit ($q > 2.0$).

Martin Kruskal & Vitaly Shafranov 1954 Kink Stability Limit

The safety factor q represents the number of toroidal turns a magnetic field line makes around the donut for every single poloidal turn; maintaining $q > 2.0$ (typically $q_{95} ge 3.0$) prevents helically resonant external kink instabilities from causing sudden multi-gigawatt thermal disruptions against the vessel wall.

Frequently asked questions

Why do tokamaks elongate plasma vertically (κ > 1.5)?

Elongation increases the edge safety factor for a given plasma current ($q propto rac{1+kappa^2}{2}$), allowing higher plasma currents ($I_p$) and superior energy confinement.
