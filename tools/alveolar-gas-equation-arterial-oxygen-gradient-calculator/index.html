How to use Alveolar Gas Equation & Alveolar-Arterial (A-a) Oxygen Gradient Calculator

  1. Enter inspired oxygen fraction $ ext{FiO}_2$ (0.21 room air, 0.40 for 40% mask, 1.0 for pure oxygen).
  2. Enter arterial oxygen partial pressure $ ext{PaO}_2$ in mmHg from an Arterial Blood Gas (ABG).
  3. Enter arterial carbon dioxide partial pressure $ ext{PaCO}_2$ in mmHg from ABG.
  4. Enter patient age in years to compute age-adjusted gradient threshold ($( ext{Age}/4) + 4$).
  5. Inspect Alveolar oxygen tension $P_A ext{O}_2$, A-a gradient, and diagnostic etiology of hypoxia.

Alveolar Gas Equation Clinical Diagnostic Standard

Differentiates intrinsic lung disease (pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, ARDS with high A-a gradient) from extrinsic central respiratory depression (opioid overdose, COPD hypoventilation with normal A-a gradient).

Frequently asked questions

Why does the A-a gradient increase with age?

Normal physiological lung aging increases closing volume and minor ventilation-perfusion ($V/Q$) mismatch, gradually widening normal A-a from $sim 8 ext{ mmHg}$ at age 20 to $sim 24 ext{ mmHg}$ at age 80.
