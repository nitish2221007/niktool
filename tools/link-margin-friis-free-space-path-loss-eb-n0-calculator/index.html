How to use Deep Space Satellite Link Budget & Free-Space Path Loss (FSPL) Calculator

  1. Enter satellite radio transmitter RF power output in Watts.
  2. Enter satellite high-gain antenna dish gain $G_{ ext{tx}}$ in dBi.
  3. Enter receiving ground station antenna dish gain $G_{ ext{rx}}$ in dBi (e.g. 65 dBi for a 70-meter deep space dish).
  4. Enter carrier radio frequency in GHz.
  5. Enter transmission distance in Millions of kilometers (e.g. 75 M km for Mars average).
  6. Inspect received power level $P_{ ext{rx}}$ in dBm and total Free-Space Path Loss (FSPL) in dB.

Harald T. Friis 1946 Free-Space Transmission Law

Quantifies inverse-square electromagnetic power spreading across interplanetary space ($P_{ ext{rx}} propto 1/d^2$), enabling NASA and ESA to size microwave amplifiers and receiver cryogenic low-noise amplifiers (LNAs).

Frequently asked questions

Why is received power measured in picowatts or femtowatts?

Across millions of kilometers, path loss exceeds $260 ext{ dB}$ ($10^{-26}$), so even a 20 Watt transmitter delivers less than $10^{-15} ext{ Watts}$ to the receiver dish.
