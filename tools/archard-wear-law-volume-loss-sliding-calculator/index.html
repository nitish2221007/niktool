How to use Archard Wear Law Sliding Volume Loss (V = K · W · s / H) Calculator

  1. Enter applied normal compressive contact load W in Newtons.

  3. Enter softer contacting material hardness H in Vickers (HV).
  4. Enter dimensionless Archard wear coefficient K (e.g. $1.0 imes 10^{-5}$ for mild lubricated wear, $1.0 imes 10^{-3}$ for severe unlubricated sliding).

John F. Archard 1953 Asperity Plastic Deformation Law

Archard proved that sliding wear volume is directly proportional to applied normal load and sliding distance while inversely proportional to material hardness ($V propto W s / H$), providing the quantitative framework for pin-on-disk wear testing and brake pad lifecycle sizing.

Frequently asked questions

Why does increasing surface hardness reduce wear so effectively?

Harder materials reduce the real contact area of micro-asperities under load ($A_{ ext{real}} = W/H$), directly decreasing the probability of adhesive debris detachment.
