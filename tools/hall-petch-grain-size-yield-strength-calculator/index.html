E.O. Hall 1951 & N.J. Petch 1953 Dislocation Pile-Up Theory

Grain boundaries act as physical barriers that block dislocation slip motion; smaller grains reduce the number of dislocations in a slip pile-up ($n propto d$), requiring higher applied stress to nucleate yielding in adjacent grains without sacrificing fracture toughness.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Inverse Hall-Petch effect?

Below ~10 nanometers grain size, grain boundary sliding takes over from dislocation slip, causing material strength to soften rather than harden.
