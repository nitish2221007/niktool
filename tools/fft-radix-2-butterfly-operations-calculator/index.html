James Cooley & John Tukey 1965 Algorithm Revolution

The FFT reduces time complexity from O(N²) to O(N log₂N) by recursively decomposing an N-point transform into interleaved even and odd sub-transforms; for a 1-million-point dataset, FFT delivers an astonishing 100,000× speedup.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Radix-2 FFT require N to be a power of 2?

Because Radix-2 recursively halves the sequence length at each stage (N -> N/2 -> N/4 ... -> 1); non-powers of two use Bluestein or mixed-radix algorithms.
