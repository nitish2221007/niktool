How to use Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz (GHK) Resting Membrane Potential (V_m) Calculator

  1. Enter extracellular $[K^+]_o$ and intracellular $[K^+]_i$ concentrations in mM (typically 4.5 mM out, 140 mM in).
  2. Enter extracellular $[Na^+]_o$ and intracellular $[Na^+]_i$ concentrations in mM (typically 145 mM out, 12 mM in).
  3. Enter relative sodium-to-potassium membrane permeability ratio $P_{Na}/P_K$ (0.04 at rest, 20.0 at peak action potential).
  4. Inspect steady-state resting membrane potential $V_m$ in millivolts and compare against individual Nernst equilibrium potentials.

David Goldman 1943 & Hodgkin-Katz 1949 Multi-Ion Electrophysiology

Because cell membranes at rest are 25× more permeable to potassium than sodium ($P_K gg P_{Na}$), the resting potential sits near the potassium Nernst potential ($E_K sim -90 ext{ mV}$); hyperkalemia (high blood potassium) depolarizes $V_m$, triggering fatal cardiac arrhythmias.

Frequently asked questions

Why are chloride concentrations flipped in the GHK equation?

Because chloride is a negatively charged anion ($z = -1$), its intracellular concentration appears in the numerator and extracellular concentration in the denominator.
