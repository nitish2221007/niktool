  1. Enter apparent visual magnitude m seen through telescopes on Earth (lower/negative is brighter).
  2. Enter absolute bolometric/visual magnitude M (brightness if placed at standard 10 parsecs).
  3. Inspect distance modulus ($mu = m - M$) and true stellar distance in parsecs and light-years.

Norman Pogson 1856 Logarithmic Stellar Brightness Scale

A difference of 5 magnitudes corresponds exactly to a $100 imes$ difference in light flux ($1 ext{ magnitude} = sqrt[5]{100} approx 2.512$), providing the foundational standard candle distance ruler for Type Ia supernovae and Cepheid variables.

Frequently asked questions

What is the absolute magnitude of our Sun?

The Sun has an absolute visual magnitude $M = +4.83$; if placed at $10 ext{ parsecs}$ ($32.6 ext{ ly}$), it would appear as a faint 5th-magnitude star barely visible to the naked eye.
