Rotational Analog of Linear Newtonian Mechanics

Moment of Inertia ($I = sum m_i r_i^2$) represents rotational inertia; flywheels store grid kinetic energy ($K_{ ext{rot}} = rac{1}{2} I omega^2$), while conservation of angular momentum ($L = Iomega = ext{constant}$) governs ice skater spins and pulsar neutron stars.

Frequently asked questions

Why does mass placed farther from the axis increase moment of inertia so dramatically?

Because moment of inertia scales with radius squared ($I propto r^2$), placing mass on outer rims quadruples rotational resistance for the same mass.
