How to use MOSFET Subthreshold Swing (SS) & Off-State Leakage Current Calculator

  1. Enter transistor operating junction temperature T in Kelvin (300 K room temp to 380 K hot chip).
  2. Enter depletion-to-oxide capacitance body factor ratio $C_{ ext{dep}}/C_{ ext{ox}}$ (typically 0.1 to 0.2 for planar MOSFETs, $sim 0.05$ for FinFETs).
  3. Enter MOSFET threshold voltage $V_{ ext{th}}$ in Volts.
  4. Inspect subthreshold swing slope $SS$ in mV/decade, theoretical Boltzmann thermal limit ($60 ext{ mV/decade}$ at 300 K), and off-state subthreshold leakage ratio.

Robert Dennard Scaling & The Boltzmann Tyranny Limit

Thermal carrier diffusion imposes a fundamental thermodynamic barrier on classical field-effect transistors: subthreshold swing cannot drop below $SS_{min} = ln(10) rac{k_B T}{q} approx 60 ext{ mV/decade}$ at room temperature, forcing static standby leakage power to rise as threshold voltages are scaled down.

Frequently asked questions

How do steep-slope devices (TFETs / NCFETs) beat the 60 mV/dec limit?

Tunnel FETs (band-to-band quantum filtering) and Negative Capacitance FETs (ferroelectric voltage amplification) bypass thermal Fermi distribution tails to achieve $SS < 60 ext{ mV/dec}$.
