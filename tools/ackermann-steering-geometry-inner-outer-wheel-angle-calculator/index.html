Georg Lankensperger & Rudolph Ackermann 1818 Geometry

During a turn, the inner front wheel traces a tighter circle than the outer wheel; Ackermann steering linkages angle the inner wheel more sharply than the outer wheel ($delta_{ ext{inner}} > delta_{ ext{outer}}$), ensuring all four wheels rotate around a single common instantaneous center of curvature to eliminate tire scrubbing.

Frequently asked questions

Why do race cars often use Parallel or Anti-Ackermann steering?

At high speeds, lateral tire slip angles dominate; high-load outer tires benefit from greater steering angles to maximize aerodynamic cornering grip.
