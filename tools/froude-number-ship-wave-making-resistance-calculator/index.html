William Froude 1868 Hydrodynamic Scaling Law

As a displacement hull approaches $Fn approx 0.40$ ($ ext{SLR} approx 1.34$), the transverse bow wave wavelength matches the ship's waterline length, trapping the vessel in a deep wave trough that creates an exponential wave-making drag barrier unless the hull transitions to dynamic planing.

Frequently asked questions

How do long slender ships (e.g. destroyers / catamarans) exceed hull speed?

Slender hulls with length-to-beam ratios $> 8:1$ generate very thin bow wave patterns, reducing wave-making drag and allowing displacement cruising past $Fn = 0.50$.
