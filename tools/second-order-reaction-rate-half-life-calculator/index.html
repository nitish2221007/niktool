Concentration-Dependent Half-Life Doubling

In second-order reactions, molecular collisions govern the speed; as reactants are consumed and become dilute, each successive half-life takes exactly twice as long as the preceding one ($t_{1/2} = 1/(kcdot[A]_0)$).

Frequently asked questions

Why does second-order half-life double each cycle?

Because $[A]$ is cut in half, $1/(kcdot [A])$ doubles, causing reactions to slow dramatically at low concentrations.
