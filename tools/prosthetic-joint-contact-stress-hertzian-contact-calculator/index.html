How to use Orthopedic Prosthetic Hip Joint Hertzian Contact Stress & Wear Calculator

  1. Enter peak hip joint resultant contact force F in Newtons (during walking gait, peak force reaches 3–4× body weight, $sim 2500 ext{ N}$).
  2. Enter prosthetic femoral head ball radius $R_1$ in mm (e.g. 14 mm for 28mm head, 16 mm for 32mm head, 18 mm for 36mm head).
  3. Enter acetabular socket cup inner radius $R_2$ in mm ($R_2 > R_1$).
  4. Enter liner material Young's Elastic Modulus in GPa ($1.0 ext{ GPa}$ for UHMWPE plastic, $400 ext{ GPa}$ for Alumina ceramic).
  5. Inspect peak compressive contact stress $sigma_{max}$ in MPa and contact patch footprint size.

Frequently asked questions

Why do larger femoral heads (36mm vs 28mm) reduce dislocation risk?

Larger heads provide greater jump distance before dislocation occurs, and spread joint contact forces over a larger surface area, lowering peak pressure.
