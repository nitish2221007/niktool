  1. Enter ambient summer dry-bulb temperature in Fahrenheit (°F) (valid for temperatures ≥ 80°F).
  2. Enter relative humidity percentage (%).
  3. Inspect NOAA Heat Index apparent temperature and OSHA workplace heat illness alert tier.

Why High Humidity Blocks Evaporative Cooling

The human body cools itself by evaporating sweat from the skin into the air. When relative humidity is high, sweat cannot evaporate efficiently, causing core body temperatures to surge dangerously.
