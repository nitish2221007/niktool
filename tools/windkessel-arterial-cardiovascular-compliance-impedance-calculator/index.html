How to use Windkessel Two-Element Arterial Compliance & Vascular Impedance Calculator

  1. Enter peak systolic aortic blood pressure $P_0$ in mmHg (typically 120 mmHg).
  2. Enter systemic Total Peripheral Resistance (TPR) R in $ ext{mmHg}cdot ext{s/mL}$ (normal $sim 1.0 ext{ mmHg}cdot ext{s/mL}$).
  3. Enter systemic arterial tree compliance C in $ ext{mL/mmHg}$ (normal $sim 1.0 ext{ to }1.5 ext{ mL/mmHg}$, decreases with age/atherosclerosis).
  4. Enter cardiac diastolic cycle duration t in seconds (typically 0.4–0.6 s at 70 bpm).
  5. Inspect end-diastolic arterial pressure decay $P(t)$ and aortic elastic buffer volume.

Otto Frank 1899 Windkessel ("Air Chamber") Cardiovascular Standard

Models the aorta as an elastic capacitor that expands during systole to absorb high pressure and recoils during diastole to maintain continuous forward capillary blood flow throughout the body.

Frequently asked questions

What happens to blood pressure when arteries stiffen with age (low C)?

Arterial compliance C drops, decreasing time constant $ au = RC$, causing systolic pressure to spike dangerously high and diastolic pressure to drop rapidly (isolated systolic hypertension).
