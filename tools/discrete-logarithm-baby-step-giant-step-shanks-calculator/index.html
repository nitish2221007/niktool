Daniel Shanks 1971 Baby-Step Giant-Step Algorithm

Shanks replaced brute-force search ($O(p)$) with a meet-in-the-middle time-memory tradeoff ($O(sqrt{p})$), proving that cryptographic key sizes must be at least twice as large as the desired security bit-level to resist square-root attacks.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the Discrete Logarithm Problem so crucial for cryptography?

Computing powers ($g^x pmod p$) is easy ($O(log x)$), but reversing it to find exponent $x$ takes astronomical time without quantum computers (Shor's algorithm).
