How to use Thermodynamic Cycles (Carnot, Otto & Diesel Thermal Efficiency) Calculator

  1. Enter engine geometric cylinder compression ratio r (e.g. 9.5 to 11.0 for petrol/gasoline engines, 16.0 to 22.0 for diesel engines).
  2. Enter working fluid ratio of specific heats $gamma = C_p / C_v$ (1.40 standard for air).
  3. Enter peak combustion temperature $T_H$ in Kelvin and ambient heat sink exhaust temperature $T_C$ in Kelvin.
  4. Inspect air-standard Otto cycle efficiency and compare against the absolute theoretical Carnot limit ($eta_{ ext{Carnot}} = 1 - T_C/T_H$).

Nicolas Léonard Sadi Carnot 1824 Second Law of Thermodynamics

No real heat engine can ever exceed the efficiency of a reversible Carnot engine operating between the same two temperature reservoirs ($eta le 1 - T_C/T_H$), establishing the fundamental limit on human energy conversion.

Frequently asked questions

Why do Diesel engines have higher thermal efficiency than Otto gasoline engines?

Diesel engines operate at much higher compression ratios ($r = 16 ext{ to }22$ vs $r = 9 ext{ to }11$) without premature knocking/autoignition, directly boosting thermal efficiency.
