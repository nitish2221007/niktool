Gary Miller & Michael Rabin 1980 Primality Standard

Unlike Fermat's test which is fooled by Carmichael numbers (like 561), Miller-Rabin guarantees that every composite number fails for at least $ rac{3}{4}$ of all possible bases, making 64 rounds of testing virtually foolproof ($< 4^{-64} approx 10^{-38}$ error rate).

Frequently asked questions

What is a Carmichael number?

A Carmichael number (like 561 = 3 × 11 × 17) is a composite number that passes Fermat's Little Theorem ($a^{n-1} equiv 1 pmod n$) for all coprime bases but is immediately caught by Miller-Rabin.
