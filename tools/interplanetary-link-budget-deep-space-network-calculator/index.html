How to use NASA Deep Space Network (DSN) Interplanetary RF Link Budget Calculator

  1. Enter spacecraft transmitter radio frequency power $P_t$ in Watts (e.g. 20 W Traveling-Wave Tube Amplifier).
  2. Enter distance from Earth in Astronomical Units (1.5 AU for Mars, 5.2 AU for Jupiter, 160 AU for Voyager 1).

Harald T. Friis 1946 Deep Space Telecommunications Law

Because electromagnetic signals expand spherically across billions of kilometers ($ ext{FSPL} propto 1/d^2$), Voyager 1's signal arrives at Earth with less than a quintillionth of a watt ($10^{-18} ext{ W} = -150 ext{ dBm}$), requiring cryogenic liquid-helium-cooled MASER preamplifiers on 70-meter parabolic dishes to recover scientific data.

Frequently asked questions

Why is NASA transitioning deep space links from X-band to Ka-band?

Ka-band (32 GHz) has 4× shorter wavelength than X-band (8.4 GHz), increasing parabolic antenna gain by $+12 ext{ dB}$ to deliver $4 imes$ to $10 imes$ higher scientific telemetry downlink speeds.
