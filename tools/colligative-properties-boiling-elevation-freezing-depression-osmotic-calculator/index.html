How to use Colligative Properties (Boiling Point Elevation, Freezing Point Depression & Osmotic Pressure) Calculator

  1. Enter van 't Hoff ionization dissociation factor i ($i=1$ for glucose, $i=2$ for $ ext{NaCl}$, $i=3$ for $ ext{CaCl}_2$).
  2. Enter solute molal concentration m in mol/kg.
  3. Enter solution temperature in $^circ ext{C}$.
  4. Inspect depressed freezing point ($T_f$), elevated boiling point ($T_b$), and generated Osmotic Pressure ($Pi$).

Solute Number-Dependent Colligative Properties

Colligative properties depend solely on the number ratio of solute particles to solvent molecules rather than chemical identity, explaining why road salt ($ ext{NaCl}$ and $ ext{CaCl}_2$) depresses the freezing point of icy roadways.

Frequently asked questions

What is the van 't Hoff factor i?

The van 't Hoff factor represents the number of discrete ions produced per formula unit of solute when dissolved in solution.
